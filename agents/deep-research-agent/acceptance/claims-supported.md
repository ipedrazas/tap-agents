A claim is a statement about the world that a reader would need a source to trust: a fact, a figure, a date, a name, what someone said or decided. These are not claims and need no citation:

- structure: titles, lead-ins and transitions ("Two further points matter.", "In short:");
- the report's own reasoning, when it follows from claims it has already cited ("So the population fell by about 6% between 2010 and 2023.");
- "> Note:" lines, where the report says what it could not find.

Read the answer and list its claims. For each one, decide:

- Supported: it carries a citation [n], and source n in the evidence (the "source" field of each page) states the claim, or something that directly entails it. Judge what the page says, not the words it uses: a faithful paraphrase is supported. Numbers, dates and names must match; a rounded figure is supported only if the answer marks it as approximate.
- Not supported: it has no citation; or the cited page is about the topic but doesn't make this claim, contradicts it, or makes a weaker or narrower claim; or the evidence has no text for that source.

Score = (supported claims) / (all claims). If there are more than 40 claims, judge the first 40. In the reason, name each claim that is not supported, quoting a few words of it, with its source number or "no citation", e.g. "[3] does not mention the 2019 figure; 'the most densely populated city in Europe' has no citation".
